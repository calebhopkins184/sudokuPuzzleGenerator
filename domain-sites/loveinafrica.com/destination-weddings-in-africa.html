Destination Weddings in Africa: How to Plan One

The first decision for any destination wedding in Africa is whether the ceremony will be legally binding or symbolic. Many couples marry legally at home and hold a symbolic ceremony abroad, which avoids foreign paperwork. If you want a legal wedding in Africa, South Africa, Mauritius and the Seychelles are among the most established options for visitors.

By the Love in Africa editorial team · Updated November 2, 2026

Legal or symbolic?

A symbolic ceremony is simpler and is what most destination couples choose. You complete the legal formalities at home — often a small civil ceremony — and the celebration abroad can take any form you like, led by a celebrant, friend or family member.

A legal ceremony abroad means meeting the host country’s marriage laws and then making sure your home country recognizes the marriage. That usually involves original documents, official translations and certifications such as apostilles, submitted weeks or months in advance.

Legal vs symbolic ceremonies
Legal abroadSymbolic abroad
PaperworkSignificant; strict deadlinesMinimal (handled at home)
Ceremony freedomMust include legally required elements and an authorized officerComplete freedom of format
Risk of delaysHigher (documents, registrar availability)Low
Recognition at homeUsually recognized if valid where performed — confirm locallyNot applicable

Popular countries for weddings

South Africa

South Africa is the most versatile wedding destination on the continent: Winelands estates, Cape Town beaches and mountains, Garden Route forests and safari lodges, with an experienced wedding industry. Foreign couples can marry legally through a designated marriage officer. South Africa is also the only African country where same-sex marriage is legal, under the Civil Union Act of 2006.

Mauritius

Mauritius is a long-established beach wedding destination with many resorts that host ceremonies. Non-residents can marry in a civil ceremony, but they must submit documents in advance and may need to be on the island for a set period before the ceremony. Resort wedding coordinators can explain the current process.

Seychelles

The Seychelles offers barefoot beach ceremonies on granite-framed coves. Non-residents may marry civilly with advance notice and documents filed with the Civil Status Office.

Kenya, Tanzania and safari settings

Safari weddings and vow renewals are increasingly popular in lodges and conservancies. Many couples hold a symbolic ceremony here. Ceremonies in national parks or on public beaches may need specific permits, so work with a local planner who handles them.

Morocco

Morocco’s riads, palaces and desert camps make stunning backdrops for celebrations. Legal marriage for foreigners can be complex, so symbolic ceremonies are common.

Documents you may need for a legal wedding

Exact requirements vary, but expect some of the following:

  • Valid passports
  • Full birth certificates
  • Proof that you are free to marry (for example, a certificate of no impediment or a sworn statement)
  • Divorce decrees or death certificates from previous marriages
  • Apostilles or legalization of documents, and certified translations
  • Advance application forms and fees paid to the registrar

Always confirm the current list with the official civil registry and your embassy, and keep certified copies of everything.

Planning timeline

  1. 12–18 months out: choose country and season, set a budget, and hire a local planner.
  2. 9–12 months: book the venue and key vendors; send save-the-dates so guests can budget for flights.
  3. 6–9 months: start legal paperwork if needed; arrange group accommodation and guest activities.
  4. 3–6 months: share entry, visa and health information with guests; finalize the ceremony format.
  5. Final month: confirm transfers, a weather backup plan and the timeline with your planner.

Looking after your guests

  • Entry rules: guests from different countries may need different visas. Point them to official government sites.
  • Health: encourage guests to see a travel clinic about vaccines and malaria advice.
  • Cost: long-haul travel is a big ask. Offer a range of accommodation options and be gracious about guests who can’t attend.
  • Turn it into a trip: many couples offer an optional safari or excursion around the wedding.

Season and weather

Match the season to the location: the Western Cape is driest in its summer (about November to March), East African safari areas in roughly June to October, and Mauritius is most comfortable from May to December. Always have a covered backup plan.

Inclusive and respectful planning

  • LGBTQ+ couples: laws differ widely. South Africa recognizes same-sex marriage. Several countries, including Botswana, Mauritius and the Seychelles, have decriminalized same-sex relations, but others criminalize them. Check official travel advisories.
  • Culture: if you want to incorporate local traditions, do so with guidance from people of that culture. See our guide to African wedding traditions.
  • Community and sustainability: hire local vendors and musicians, and ask venues about their conservation and community work.
