Asphalt Paving in Anchorage, Alaska: A Practical Homeowner's Guide

Plain-language guidance on driveway paving, sealcoating, crack filling and frost heave repair for Anchorage and Southcentral Alaska, written around the realities of freeze-thaw, breakup and a short paving season.

Asphalt in Alaska lives a hard life. Frost-susceptible soils, months of freezing temperatures, spring breakup, plows and studded tires all test pavement in ways a guide written for warmer places won't cover. This site explains how good paving is done here, what to ask contractors, and how to make pavement last. We are an independent information resource, not a paving company.

The three rules of Alaska pavement

  1. The base matters most. A thick, compacted, free-draining gravel base is what resists frost heave. See driveway paving in Anchorage.
  2. Keep water out. Water plus freezing is behind most cracking and heaving. Grade for drainage and seal cracks promptly.
  3. Respect the season. Asphalt has to be placed hot and compacted on a dry, thawed base. Plan around the Alaska paving season.

Frequently asked questions

When is asphalt paving season in Anchorage?

Residential paving generally runs from about mid-to-late May through September, depending on weather and when local asphalt plants are operating. Mid-summer is the most reliable period. More on timing.

Why do Anchorage driveways heave in winter?

Frost heave happens when water in frost-susceptible soil, such as silt, freezes into ice lenses that lift the pavement. Removing that soil and replacing it with free-draining gravel, plus good drainage, is the lasting fix.

How often should I sealcoat my driveway?

Wait until new asphalt has cured, often a full season, then reseal every few years based on wear rather than a fixed schedule. Over-sealing builds a thick layer that can peel.

How much does it cost to pave a driveway in Anchorage?

It depends heavily on base preparation, size, thickness, access and current material prices. We don't publish quotes; get several detailed written estimates and compare the scope line by line using our bid checklist.

Should I fill cracks myself?

Narrow cracks can be cleaned and filled with store-bought filler as a DIY job. Wide or working cracks last longer with professional hot-pour rubberized sealant. Alligator cracking or heaving signals a base problem that crack filling won't fix.

Do I need a permit to pave my driveway?

Work where a driveway meets a public street generally requires approval from the Municipality of Anchorage or the relevant road authority. Check current requirements before work starts, and always call 811 before digging.
