Sealcoating Asphalt in Alaska: Timing, Weather and What It Really Does

Last updated November 2, 2026

Sealcoating is a thin protective coat, usually a refined coal-tar-free asphalt emulsion or acrylic product, spread over existing asphalt. It slows oxidation from sun and air, sheds water, resists fuel and oil spills, and restores a dark appearance. It does not add strength, and it can't fix cracks, potholes or heaving.

Is sealcoating worth it in Alaska?

Sealcoating is worth it when the pavement is structurally sound and you want to slow surface aging and water intrusion. It is not worth paying for on pavement that is already badly cracked or heaving; that money is better spent on crack repair or replacement.

Asphalt binder slowly oxidizes and hardens, which makes the surface brittle and gray and lets small aggregate ravel out. A sealer slows that process. In Alaska, the more important benefit is often water management: a tight surface lets less meltwater into the pavement, and less water means less freeze-thaw damage.

What sealcoat won't do:

  • Bridge or repair cracks wider than a hairline. Those need proper crack filling first.
  • Stand up to studded tires and plow blades for long on high-traffic areas; expect those zones to wear first.
  • Correct drainage, settling or base failure.

When should new asphalt be sealed?

Most manufacturers and contractors recommend waiting until new asphalt has cured, commonly several months and often a full season, before applying the first sealcoat. Sealing too early can trap the lighter oils that are still leaving the fresh pavement.

After the first coat, resealing every few years is common for residential driveways, depending on traffic, sun exposure and how quickly the surface grays. Applying it too often builds up a thick layer that can crack and peel, so don't reseal on a fixed calendar if the existing coat is still in good shape.

The weather window for sealcoating in Anchorage

Sealcoat products are water-based emulsions that need warmth and dry weather to cure. Requirements vary by product, so the data sheet governs, but typical conditions include:

Air and pavement temperatureAt or above about 50°F and rising, staying above that during cureCool evenings in May and September can stop curing even after a warm afternoon
RainNo rain in the forecast for roughly 24 hours or moreSummer showers are common; rain on fresh sealer can wash it off
Sunlight and humiditySun and low humidity speed curingLong June and July days help; overcast, damp coastal days slow cure
TrafficUsually kept off for about 24-48 hoursPlan around work schedules and deliveries

In practice, the most reliable sealcoating weather in Anchorage tends to come in mid-summer. Late-season jobs risk cold nights and early rain. Ask the contractor what product they use and request its technical data sheet if you want to check the application limits yourself.

How a proper sealcoat job is done

  1. Cleaning. Dirt, gravel, moss and loose material are removed with brooms, blowers or power washing. Sealer applied over dust doesn't bond.
  2. Oil spot treatment. Oil and fuel stains are cleaned and primed, or the sealer will lift there.
  3. Crack filling. Cracks are cleaned and filled with an appropriate crack sealant before coating.
  4. Application. Sealer is applied by squeegee, brush or spray, commonly in two thin coats rather than one thick coat. Sand is often mixed in for traction and wear.
  5. Cure. The area is barricaded until it is ready for traffic.

Coal tar vs. asphalt emulsion vs. acrylic sealers

Coal-tar-based sealers have been restricted or banned in a number of U.S. states and cities because of concerns about polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) washing into waterways and tracking into homes. Asphalt-emulsion and acrylic/polymer sealers are widely available alternatives. Given Anchorage's salmon streams and storm drains that lead to them, choosing a coal-tar-free product is a sensible default. Ask what's in the product being quoted.

DIY or hire it out?

Homeowners can sealcoat a small driveway with store-bought products, a squeegee and patience. The common DIY mistakes are skipping cleaning, applying one thick coat, sealing over unfilled cracks and coating too close to rain or a cold night. A contractor brings commercial-grade material and equipment and can usually finish in a few hours, but you are still responsible for confirming the weather and cure times fit your schedule.

Frequently asked questions

How soon can I seal a new asphalt driveway?

Wait until the asphalt has cured, which is commonly several months and often a full season. Your paving contractor can tell you what they recommend for the mix they used.

Can you sealcoat asphalt in September in Anchorage?

Sometimes, but it's risky. Sealers generally need temperatures around 50°F or higher during cure plus dry weather, and September nights in Anchorage are often colder than that. Mid-summer is the more dependable window.

Does sealcoating fill cracks?

No. Sealcoat is a thin surface coat. Cracks should be cleaned and filled with a dedicated crack sealant before sealcoating.
