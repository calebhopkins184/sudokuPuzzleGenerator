Asphalt Paving Cost Factors and How to Choose a Contractor in Anchorage

Last updated November 2, 2026

There is no honest single price for asphalt paving in Anchorage. Cost depends on how much base work your site needs, the size and shape of the area, access, asphalt thickness, and the current price of materials and fuel. Understanding those factors is the best way to compare bids and avoid paying for a cheap job twice.

This site doesn't publish price quotes. Material and labor costs change from season to season. Get written estimates from several local contractors for your specific property.

What affects the cost of an asphalt driveway?

The biggest cost drivers are excavation and base preparation, total area, asphalt thickness, site access and complexity, and market prices for asphalt binder and fuel. Base work often varies the most from one property to the next.

FactorWhy it changes the price
Excavation and baseRemoving frost-susceptible soil and hauling in gravel is labor- and truck-intensive. Sites with deep silt or poor drainage need much more of it.
Area and shapeLarger areas usually cost less per square foot because mobilization is spread out. Curves, tight corners and hand work raise costs.
Asphalt thicknessMore thickness means more tons of hot mix. Driveways that see RVs, trucks or equipment need a thicker section.
Removal of old pavementTear-out and disposal add cost compared with paving over gravel or overlaying sound asphalt.
Access and logisticsSteep slopes, narrow access, long hauls from the plant or remote locations (outside the Anchorage bowl) add time and trucking.
Drainage featuresCulverts, swales, regrading around the house or edge treatments add materials and labor.
Season and scheduleDemand peaks in summer. Asphalt prices track oil prices, and some contractors adjust pricing over the season.
PermitsRight-of-way or other permits and related work at the street connection can add cost.

How to compare paving bids

Compare bids line by line on scope, not just the bottom number. A lower bid that excavates less, uses thinner asphalt or skips compaction steps is not the same job.

Ask every contractor to put these in writing:

  • Area to be paved (square feet or square yards) and a sketch if the shape is irregular.
  • Excavation depth and how unsuitable soil will be handled.
  • Base material type, depth, and compaction method.
  • Compacted asphalt thickness and mix type.
  • Drainage plan: slope direction and how water leaves the driveway.
  • Edge treatment and how the driveway meets the street and garage slab.
  • Who obtains any permits, and who calls 811 for utility locates.
  • Cleanup, restoration of disturbed lawn or landscaping, and payment schedule.
  • Warranty terms: what is covered, for how long, and what voids it.

How do I choose a paving contractor in Anchorage?

Choose a contractor who is properly registered and insured in Alaska, explains the base and drainage work in specific terms, gives a detailed written estimate, and can point you to recent local work you can look at in person.

Verify registration and insurance

Alaska requires construction contractors to register with the state Division of Corporations, Business and Professional Licensing, which involves a bond and insurance requirements. You can search the division's online license and registration database to confirm a business is currently registered. Ask for a certificate of insurance showing liability coverage, and confirm workers' compensation coverage if they have employees.

Watch for common red flags

  • Door-to-door crews offering "leftover asphalt from another job" at a discount, especially with pressure to decide immediately.
  • Requests for large cash payments up front.
  • No written estimate, or an estimate with no details on base or thickness.
  • Unmarked trucks, no verifiable business name, or reluctance to show registration.
  • Promises that skip base work entirely ("we'll just pave right over it") on a site with known heaving.

Look at real work

Ask for addresses of driveways the contractor paved a few years ago, not just last month. Older work reveals how the base and drainage held up through several Anchorage winters. You can also check the Better Business Bureau and online reviews, keeping in mind that any single review can be unreliable.

Ways to control cost without cutting corners

  • Book early. Contractors' schedules fill up for the short season; getting estimates in late winter or early spring gives you more choice.
  • Coordinate with neighbors. Some contractors can reduce mobilization costs when several nearby driveways are paved the same week.
  • Prioritize base over extras. Decorative edges are optional; base depth and drainage aren't.
  • Maintain what you have. Timely crack filling and occasional sealcoating are far cheaper than early replacement.

Frequently asked questions

Why do paving estimates in Anchorage vary so much?

Mostly because contractors propose different amounts of excavation, base gravel and asphalt thickness. Compare the scope line by line; the lowest bid may simply be a thinner, shallower job.

How can I check if an Alaska contractor is registered?

Search the Alaska Division of Corporations, Business and Professional Licensing online database for the business name, and ask the contractor for a current certificate of insurance.
