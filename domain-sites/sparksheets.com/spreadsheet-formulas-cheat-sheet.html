Essential Excel and Google Sheets formulas cheat sheet

Updated November 2, 2026 · By the SparkSheets team

These are the formulas that cover most everyday spreadsheet work. Each works in both Microsoft Excel and Google Sheets unless noted. Formulas always start with an equals sign, and arguments are separated by commas — though in some regional settings, such as many European locales, the separator is a semicolon.

Cell references: relative, absolute, and mixed

ReferenceWhen copiedUse it for
A1Column and row both shiftMost formulas
$A$1Nothing shiftsA fixed input, like a tax rate cell
$A1Row shifts, column staysChecking one column across rows
A$1Column shifts, row staysReading a header row

While editing a reference, press F4 in Excel for Windows or Google Sheets to cycle through these (on some laptops, Fn+F4; in Excel for Mac, Cmd+T).

Conditional math

FormulaWhat it does
COUNTIF(range, criteria)Counts cells meeting one condition
COUNTIFS(range1, crit1, range2, crit2, ...)Counts rows meeting all conditions
SUMIF(range, criteria, sum_range)Adds values meeting one condition
SUMIFS(sum_range, range1, crit1, ...)Adds values meeting all conditions
AVERAGEIFS(avg_range, range1, crit1, ...)Averages values meeting all conditions

Note that SUMIF puts the sum range last, while SUMIFS puts it first.

=COUNTIF(C2:C200, "Paid")
=SUMIFS(D2:D200, B2:B200, "West", C2:C200, "Paid")
=SUMIFS(D2:D200, A2:A200, ">="&DATE(2026,1,1), A2:A200, "<"&DATE(2027,1,1))
=COUNTIF(A2:A200, "*widget*")

Criteria with operators go in quotes and join to cell references with &. The * wildcard matches any characters.

Logic

=IF(B2>=100, "Target met", "Below target")
=IF(AND(B2>=100, C2="Yes"), "Bonus", "No bonus")
=IF(OR(D2="Urgent", E2<TODAY()), "Act now", "")
=IFS(B2>=90, "A", B2>=80, "B", B2>=70, "C", TRUE, "Below C")
=IFERROR(B2/C2, 0)
=IFNA(XLOOKUP(A2, F:F, G:G), "Missing")

IFS checks conditions in order and returns the first match; the final TRUE acts as "everything else." It is available in Excel 2019 and later and in Google Sheets. SWITCH is a tidy alternative for matching one value against a list:

=SWITCH(A2, "N", "North", "S", "South", "E", "East", "W", "West", "Unknown")

Lookups

=XLOOKUP(A2, Products!A:A, Products!D:D, "Not found")
=VLOOKUP(A2, Products!A:D, 4, FALSE)
=INDEX(Products!D:D, MATCH(A2, Products!A:A, 0))

Full explanations and troubleshooting are in XLOOKUP vs VLOOKUP.

Split text by a delimiter with =TEXTSPLIT(A2, ",") in Excel for Microsoft 365, or =SPLIT(A2, ",") in Google Sheets.

Dates

Spreadsheets store dates as serial numbers, so you can add and subtract them.

=TODAY()                          Current date (updates daily)
=NOW()                            Current date and time
=DATE(2026, 11, 2)                Builds a date from year, month, day
=EOMONTH(A2, 0)                   Last day of A2's month
=EDATE(A2, 3)                     Same day, 3 months later
=A2+30                            30 days after A2
=B2-A2                            Days between two dates
=NETWORKDAYS(A2, B2)              Weekdays between two dates
=WORKDAY(A2, 10)                  10 working days after A2
=DATEDIF(A2, TODAY(), "Y")        Whole years between dates (e.g., age)
=TEXT(A2, "mmmm yyyy")            Month name and year, like "November 2026"
=WEEKDAY(A2, 2)                   1 = Monday ... 7 = Sunday

DATEDIF works in both apps, although Excel does not list it in its formula autocomplete.

Dynamic arrays

These return multiple results that "spill" into neighboring cells. They are available in Excel for Microsoft 365, Excel 2021 and later, and Google Sheets.

=UNIQUE(C2:C200)                          List of distinct values
=SORT(A2:D200, 4, -1)                     Sort by the 4th column, descending
=FILTER(A2:D200, C2:C200="West")          Only rows where column C is West
=FILTER(A2:D200, (C2:C200="West")*(D2:D200>500))   Two conditions (AND)
=SORT(UNIQUE(C2:C200))                    Sorted distinct list
=SEQUENCE(12)                             Numbers 1 through 12

In Excel, a #SPILL! error means something is blocking the cells where results need to go. In Google Sheets, the equivalent error message says the array result was not expanded because it would overwrite data.

Cleaner formulas with LET

LET names intermediate values so long formulas are easier to read. It works in Excel for Microsoft 365, Excel 2021 and later, and Google Sheets.

=LET(total, SUM(B2:B50), count, COUNT(B2:B50), IF(count=0, 0, total/count))
