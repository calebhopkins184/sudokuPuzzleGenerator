Stock market basics: your beginner's playbook

Investing in stocks is easier to understand once you know the plays. A stock is a small ownership stake in a company; its price moves as investors weigh that company's future profits. Most beginners do best with a simple game plan: learn the vocabulary, keep costs low, diversify broadly with funds, and give investments years — not days — to work.

StockQB breaks the market down play by play. Start with how to read a quote, learn how to judge whether a price is expensive, understand the main fund types and order types, then put it together in a beginner portfolio.

Pre-game checklist before you buy your first stock

Before you place your first trade, run through these basics. They matter more than any single stock pick.

  • Emergency savings in place. Money you might need within a few years belongs in savings, not stocks.
  • High-interest debt under control. Paying off a credit card charging over 20% is a guaranteed return.
  • The right account. Retirement accounts like a 401(k) or IRA offer tax advantages; a taxable brokerage account offers flexibility.
  • A plan for downturns. Broad stock markets have fallen 20% or more many times. Decide now that you will not panic-sell.
  • Know the costs. Look at fund expense ratios, account fees, and bid-ask spreads.

Frequently asked questions

How much money do I need to start investing in stocks?

Many brokerages have no account minimum and offer fractional shares, so you can start with a few dollars. What matters more is investing regularly over time and keeping costs low.

Is it better to buy individual stocks or funds?

For most beginners, diversified funds such as broad index ETFs or mutual funds are the simpler starting point because one purchase spreads money across hundreds of companies. Individual stocks concentrate risk in a few businesses.

What does P/E ratio mean?

The price-to-earnings ratio is a stock's price divided by its earnings per share. It tells you how many dollars investors are paying for each dollar of annual profit. A P/E of 20 means investors pay $20 per $1 of earnings.

What is the difference between a market order and a limit order?

A market order buys or sells right away at the best available price, but the exact price is not guaranteed. A limit order sets the maximum price you will pay or the minimum you will accept, but it may not fill.

When does the stock market open?

The major U.S. exchanges, the New York Stock Exchange and Nasdaq, hold regular trading from 9:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. Eastern Time on weekdays, except market holidays. Many brokers also offer limited pre-market and after-hours trading.
