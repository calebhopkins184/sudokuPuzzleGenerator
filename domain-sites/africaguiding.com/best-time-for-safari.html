Best Time for Safari in Africa, Country by Country

For most of East and Southern Africa, the best time for a classic game-viewing safari is the local dry season, roughly June to October. Vegetation thins, animals gather at rivers and waterholes, and roads are easier. But the “green season” has real advantages too — newborn animals, superb birding, dramatic skies and lower rates — and a few places flip the usual rules entirely.

By the Africa Guiding editorial team · Updated November 2, 2026

Why the dry season is usually best

Dry-season safaris are easier because water is scarce, so wildlife becomes predictable. Elephants, buffalo and antelope must drink daily, and predators follow them. Grass is shorter, so a lion lying 30 meters from the track is actually visible. Lower humidity and fewer mosquitoes are a bonus.

The trade-offs: dry season is peak season, so lodges fill months ahead and rates are highest; parks can be dusty; and the most famous areas get busier. Mornings and evenings in Southern Africa’s winter (June to August) can be genuinely cold on an open vehicle — often near freezing in Botswana’s Kalahari and Namibia’s interior.

The green season, honestly assessed

The green (rainy) season is a good choice if you value photography, birds and value for money over guaranteed big-game density. Rain in much of Africa falls as afternoon storms rather than all-day drizzle. Landscapes turn lush, many antelope give birth, migratory birds arrive from Europe and Asia, and many lodges offer lower “green season” rates.

Downsides are real, too: some roads become impassable, some camps (notably in Zambia and parts of Zimbabwe and Tanzania) close completely, animals disperse because water is everywhere, and malaria risk is generally higher during and after the rains.

East Africa: Kenya and Tanzania

In Kenya and Tanzania, the best months are June to October, with January and February as a strong second window. East Africa sits near the equator, so it has two rainy seasons instead of one: the “long rains” (roughly March or April to May) and the “short rains” (around November).

  • Masai Mara and northern Serengeti: July to October for the Great Migration and its river crossings.
  • Southern Serengeti and Ndutu: January to March for wildebeest calving and the predator action that comes with it.
  • Tarangire: best late in the dry season (around August to October), when huge elephant herds concentrate along the Tarangire River.
  • Ngorongoro Crater: good year-round because the crater floor has permanent water.

April and May are the quietest months; some camps close, but those that stay open can be excellent value. See our Great Migration guide for month-by-month herd locations.

Southern Africa: Botswana, Zambia, Zimbabwe and Namibia

Southern Africa has a single rainy season in the southern-hemisphere summer (about November to March), so its prime safari window runs from about June to October, getting hotter and drier — and often better for game — as it goes.

  • Botswana’s Okavango Delta is the great exception to “dry means less water.” Rain that falls in the Angolan highlands takes months to flow south, so the delta’s floods typically peak around June to August — the middle of Botswana’s dry season. That is when mokoro (dugout canoe) trips are at their best.
  • Zambia’s South Luangwa and Lower Zambezi are classic dry-season destinations; September and October are hot but superb. South Luangwa is famous as a home of the walking safari.
  • Zimbabwe’s Hwange and Mana Pools peak from about July to October. Mana Pools is known for walking and canoeing.
  • Namibia’s Etosha works like a natural hide: in the dry months, animals queue at floodlit and roadside waterholes.

South Africa

South Africa is the most flexible safari country: Kruger and its neighboring private reserves can be visited year-round. The dry winter (May to September) gives the easiest game viewing and cooler days; summer (November to March) is hot, green and full of baby animals and migrant birds. A useful bonus is that the Western Cape has the opposite climate — dry summers, wet winters — so a summer safari pairs well with Cape Town.

Rwanda and Uganda

Gorilla and chimpanzee trekking in Rwanda and Uganda happen all year. The drier months (roughly June to September and December to February) make forest trails less muddy, while wetter months can mean fewer visitors. Expect rain at any time in a rainforest. Our gorilla trekking guide covers permits and fitness.

How to choose your month

  1. Pick the experience first. River crossings, calving, gorillas, mokoro trips and walking safaris each have a best window.
  2. Match the country to your dates. If you can only travel in December, South Africa, the southern Serengeti or Rwanda and Uganda may suit you better than Zambia.
  3. Book early for peak season. Small camps in prime areas often fill a year ahead for July to September.
  4. Consider shoulder months. June and November can offer a good balance of wildlife, weather and price.

Remember: seasons are patterns, not promises. Rains arrive early or late, and herds move with the grass. A flexible itinerary with a knowledgeable local operator matters more than the calendar.

Quick answers

What is the single best month for an African safari?

If you want one answer, September: it is dry across most of East and Southern Africa, wildlife is concentrated, and the Great Migration is usually in the Masai Mara and northern Serengeti. The trade-off is peak-season demand and pricing.

Is it worth going on safari in the rainy season?

Often, yes. The green season brings newborn animals, excellent birding and lower rates. Choose places that stay open and accessible, such as South Africa’s Kruger area or Tanzania’s southern Serengeti from January to March.
