Where to start

Start with your must-see experience, then work backward to the country and month. Wildlife, weather and prices all follow seasons, so the order you decide things matters.

  1. Choose the experience. Big cats and the migration? Gorillas? Quiet walking safaris? Elephants in the Okavango?
  2. Check the timing. Our best time for safari guide maps seasons for nine countries, and the Great Migration guide shows where the herds usually are each month.
  3. Pick a style. Compare lodges, tented camps, mobile camps and self-drive in safari types compared.
  4. Prepare well. Use the packing list and travel health guide, and if primates are on your list, read the gorilla trekking guide.

Safari countries in one line each

  • Kenya: the Masai Mara, private conservancies and classic savanna; easy flights from Nairobi.
  • Tanzania: the Serengeti, Ngorongoro Crater and Tarangire, often finished on Zanzibar’s beaches.
  • Botswana: the Okavango Delta, low-density luxury camps and fly-in safaris.
  • South Africa: Kruger and private reserves; the easiest self-drive and family options.
  • Zambia and Zimbabwe: walking safaris, the Zambezi and Victoria Falls.
  • Namibia: deserts, Etosha’s waterholes and long road trips.
  • Rwanda and Uganda: mountain gorillas, chimpanzees and lush highlands.

Frequently asked questions

When is the best time to go on safari in Africa?

For most of East and Southern Africa, the dry season from about June to October offers the easiest game viewing. The green season (roughly November to April) brings newborn animals, great birding and lower rates, though some camps close.

Which African country is best for a first safari?

Kenya, Tanzania and South Africa are the most popular first-safari choices. South Africa is easiest logistically and suits families; Kenya and Tanzania offer the Great Migration and classic savanna scenery.

How many days do you need for a safari?

Plan at least four to five nights on safari, ideally split between no more than two or three camps. Wildlife sightings improve with time, and fewer moves mean less time in transit.

Is an African safari safe?

Guided safaris with reputable operators are generally very safe when you follow your guide’s rules: stay in the vehicle unless told otherwise and never walk unescorted in unfenced camps at night. Check government travel advisories for each country.

Do I need vaccines for a safari?

Usually yes. Requirements depend on your route; yellow fever proof may be required for some countries, and malaria prevention is often advised. See a travel clinic four to six weeks before travel and check CDC destination pages.

Does Africa Guiding sell safaris?

No. Africa Guiding is an independent information site. We don’t sell tours, take bookings or issue permits. Book with licensed operators or official wildlife authorities.
