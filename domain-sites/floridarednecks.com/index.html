Florida Country Living: Fishing, Swamps, Southern Food and Storm Prep

Beyond the theme parks and beach condos, there's a whole other Florida: bass lakes, cypress swamps, cattle ranches, fish fries and folks who know how to ride out a storm. Florida Rednecks is a friendly, practical guide to that Florida, written with pride and respect for the people, places and wildlife that make it special.

Disclaimer: Fishing and hunting rules, seasons, bag limits, license requirements and fees change often. Always check current regulations with the Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission (FWC) before you go, and follow official emergency management guidance during storms.

What we mean by "redneck"

Here, "redneck" means a love of the outdoors, hard work, good food and helping your neighbors, not a stereotype. Florida's country folks include cattle ranchers, commercial fishermen, hunters, farmers, airboat captains and plenty of people who just like a cold drink on the tailgate after a day on the water. Everybody's welcome.

Frequently asked questions

Where's the best fishing in Florida?

For freshwater bass, try Lake Okeechobee, the Kissimmee Chain and Rodman Reservoir. For saltwater, Mosquito Lagoon for redfish, Boca Grande for tarpon, the Keys for bonefish and permit, and the Panhandle for snapper.

Where do I buy a Florida fishing or hunting license?

Through FWC at GoOutdoorsFlorida.com, the Fish|Hunt Florida app, county tax collector offices or authorized retailers. Check FWC for current fees.

Are boiled peanuts a Florida thing?

They're a Southern thing that Florida loves. You'll find them in roadside kettles across north and central Florida, and they're easy to make at home with raw peanuts, salt and time.

Is it safe to ride an airboat?

Commercial airboat tours are generally safe when you follow the operator's rules: stay seated, wear hearing protection and secure loose items. Florida requires commercial operators to complete approved training.

How should rural Floridians prepare for hurricanes?

Plan for longer outages: store at least seven days of water and supplies, run generators at least 20 feet from the house, protect your well, make a livestock plan and buy flood insurance early.

Is this site affiliated with FWC?

No. Florida Rednecks is an independent site. For official regulations, seasons and fees, always go straight to FWC.
