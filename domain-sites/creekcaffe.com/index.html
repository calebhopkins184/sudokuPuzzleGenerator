Three upgrades that matter most

  1. Fresh whole beans. Look for a roast date and use beans within a few weeks. See beans and grind size.
  2. A burr grinder. Even particles mean even extraction and a sweeter cup.
  3. A scale. Weighing coffee and water makes every good cup repeatable.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best coffee-to-water ratio?

A good general starting point is 1:16 (1 gram of coffee to 16 grams of water) for pour-over and drip, and about 1:15 for French press. Adjust stronger or weaker to taste.

What water temperature is best for brewing coffee?

About 90 to 96 degrees Celsius (195 to 205 degrees Fahrenheit). In practice, let boiling water rest for about 30 seconds before brewing.

Do I really need a burr grinder?

It is the single biggest equipment upgrade for most home brewers. Burr grinders produce even particles, which extract evenly and taste sweeter and clearer than blade-ground coffee.

How do I make good coffee while camping?

For backpacking, use coffee bags, instant coffee, an AeroPress or a collapsible pour-over cone. For car camping, a stainless French press or percolator works well. Always pack out your grounds.

Why does my coffee taste bitter?

Bitterness usually means over-extraction: the grind is too fine, the brew ran too long or the water was very hot. Try grinding coarser first.

How long do coffee beans stay fresh?

Whole beans are usually at their best from a few days to about four weeks after roasting when stored airtight away from heat and light. Ground coffee goes stale much faster.
