Alaska protective coatings guide

Protective Coatings in Alaska: Floors, Roofs, Foam, Wood and Metal

How to choose and apply coatings that survive cold slabs, freeze-thaw, coastal salt and long summer UV, from epoxy garage floors to log home stains.

Most coating failures in Alaska come down to the same few causes: poor surface preparation, moisture, and applying products outside their temperature limits. AK Coating explains how to get those right for the most common residential and light commercial projects. We are an independent educational resource and do not sell or install products.

Three rules that apply to almost every coating

  1. Preparation decides adhesion. Clean, profile and dry the surface. A premium product on a poorly prepared surface fails like a cheap one.
  2. Read the technical data sheet. It lists minimum temperatures, humidity limits, recoat windows and film thickness. Those numbers matter more than marketing claims.
  3. Watch surface temperature and dew point. In Alaska, surfaces often stay colder than the air, and evening dew arrives fast. Keep surfaces at least 5°F above the dew point.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best garage floor coating for Alaska?

Both epoxy and polyaspartic systems can work. Polyaspartics tolerate colder slabs and cure faster; epoxy is often less expensive but needs warmer conditions. Preparation matters most. Compare them.

Can coatings be applied in an Alaska winter?

Only indoors in a space where the surface itself can be kept within the product's temperature and humidity limits. Exterior coating work in most of Alaska is a summer job.

Is closed-cell or open-cell spray foam better in Alaska?

Closed-cell is more common in Alaska because it offers higher R-value per inch and acts as a vapor retarder at typical thicknesses. Open-cell usually needs a separate vapor retarder in climate zones 7 and 8.

How often do log homes need restaining?

It varies with sun exposure and product. Sunny south and west walls usually need it first. Recoat when water stops beading on the surface rather than on a fixed schedule.

Will a roof coating fix ice dams?

No. Ice dams are caused by heat loss into the roof. Air sealing, insulation and ventilation address the cause.

What's the most important step in stopping rust?

Surface preparation, including washing off salts with fresh water, followed by a rust-inhibitive primer and a durable topcoat applied above the dew point.
