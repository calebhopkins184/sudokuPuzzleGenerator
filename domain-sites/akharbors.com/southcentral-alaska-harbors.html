Southcentral Alaska Harbors: Homer, Seward, Whittier and Valdez

Last updated November 2, 2026

Homer, Seward, Whittier and Valdez are the four main small-boat harbors that Southcentral Alaska boaters can reach by road. Each is run by its city, each opens onto very different water, and each has its own access quirks, from Whittier's tunnel schedule to Valdez's long drive and Homer's big tides.

AK Harbors does not publish moorage rates, fees or availability. They change, and each harbor sets its own. Always confirm details directly with the harbor office before you go.

Homer: the end of the road on Kachemak Bay

Homer's harbor sits near the tip of the Homer Spit, a long gravel bar reaching into Kachemak Bay. It is one of the busiest harbors in the region in summer, with charter, commercial and private boats, and it has some of the largest tides of any Alaska harbor you can drive to.

  • Tides: Lower Cook Inlet tides are large, and big spring tides can exceed 20 feet of range. Plan launches, retrievals and any trip into shallow water with a tide table (see Alaska tides for boaters).
  • Water: Inner Kachemak Bay offers sheltered cruising to places like Halibut Cove and Kachemak Bay State Park. Outside the bay, lower Cook Inlet can build steep seas fast, especially when wind opposes the current.
  • Summer crowds: Expect a busy launch ramp and parking during peak fishing weeks and holiday weekends.

Seward: deep water on Resurrection Bay

Seward's small-boat harbor sits at the head of Resurrection Bay, a deep fjord that gives access to Kenai Fjords National Park and the Gulf of Alaska. It's a popular base for fishing, wildlife cruising and longer coastal trips.

  • Bay vs. Gulf: Resurrection Bay can be calm while the open Gulf beyond the bay mouth is rough. Check the marine forecast for the outside waters, not just the bay.
  • Kenai Fjords: Trips toward Aialik Bay and beyond involve exposed headlands and long distances from help. Leave a float plan.
  • Glacial outflow: Freshwater and silt from glaciers and rivers affect visibility and can carry debris after heavy rain.

Whittier: Prince William Sound via the tunnel

Whittier is the closest harbor to Anchorage for reaching Prince William Sound, but the only road in is the Anton Anderson Memorial Tunnel, a single-lane tunnel shared with the Alaska Railroad. Traffic alternates direction on a schedule, and the tunnel closes overnight, so boaters must plan both arrival and departure around it.

  • Check the schedule: The Alaska Department of Transportation & Public Facilities publishes tunnel hours, which vary by season. Missing the last opening means staying in Whittier overnight.
  • Towing: Trailered boats go through the tunnel; tolls differ by vehicle type. Check current rules for vehicle and trailer size and any restrictions on fuel or hazardous materials.
  • Weather: Whittier is known for heavy rain and strong winds funneling through Passage Canal. Fog and low clouds are common.
  • Destinations: Western Prince William Sound has many glaciers, fjords and protected anchorages, plus ice near tidewater glaciers.

Valdez: northeastern Prince William Sound

Valdez sits on Port Valdez at the end of the Richardson Highway, a long drive from Anchorage. Its harbor serves fishing and recreational boaters heading into the eastern and central Sound.

  • Ice: Icebergs and brash ice from the Columbia Glacier area can drift into shipping lanes and nearby waters. Keep a lookout, especially in poor visibility.
  • Valdez Narrows: The narrow entrance to Port Valdez has stronger currents than the open sound. Large tankers serving the Valdez Marine Terminal also use the area; monitor VHF and give them room.
  • The drive: Thompson Pass on the Richardson Highway sees severe weather and heavy snow outside summer. Check road conditions before towing.

Before you head to any of these harbors

  1. Call or check the harbor's website for transient availability, launch ramp status and parking.
  2. Review the National Weather Service marine forecast for the zones you plan to use.
  3. Print or download tide predictions from NOAA for the nearest station.
  4. Confirm fuel availability and hours at the harbor fuel dock.
  5. File a float plan with someone ashore who will act if you're overdue.

Frequently asked questions

Which Southcentral harbor is closest to Anchorage?

Whittier is the closest by road, but access depends on the Anton Anderson Memorial Tunnel schedule. Seward is reachable without a tunnel. Homer and Valdez are longer drives.

Can I take a boat trailer through the Whittier tunnel?

Yes, vehicles towing boats use the tunnel. Tolls and rules depend on vehicle type and size, so check the Alaska DOT&PF tunnel information before you go.
