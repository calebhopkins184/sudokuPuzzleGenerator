Alaska at a glance

Alaska is the largest U.S. state by area, more than twice the size of Texas, with fewer than a million residents. Most people live in Southcentral Alaska around Anchorage and the Mat-Su Valley, in the Interior around Fairbanks, and in Southeast around Juneau. Many communities aren't connected to the road system at all.

Quick facts for newcomers

Largest cityAnchorage, home to roughly 40% of Alaskans

Aurora seasonRoughly late August to mid-April, when nights are dark
Time zoneMost of the state uses Alaska Time, one hour behind Pacific Time

A lot of Alaska information online is written for one-week tourists. AK Circle focuses on what people who live here, or want to, actually need: realistic costs, residency steps, how to keep a car running at 20 below, and where to find community and daylight in December. Frequently asked questions

Is it expensive to live in Alaska?

Generally yes, especially for groceries, goods and heating, and costs rise sharply in rural communities off the road system. Alaska has no state income tax or statewide sales tax, and eligible residents receive the annual Permanent Fund Dividend, which offsets some costs.

Can you drive to Alaska?

Yes. The Alaska Highway runs through British Columbia and the Yukon, so you'll cross the Canadian border and need proper travel documents. Juneau and most rural communities can't be reached by road.

When is the best time to see the northern lights in Alaska?

From late August to mid-April on clear, dark nights, usually between about 10 p.m. and 2 a.m. Fairbanks and areas farther north offer the most frequent displays.

How cold does it get in Anchorage?

Anchorage winters are milder than many expect because of the nearby ocean. Typical winter days are in the teens to 20s °F, with occasional colder snaps. Fairbanks and the Interior are much colder, often reaching −20°F to −40°F.

How long do you have to live in Alaska to get the PFD?

Generally a full calendar year as a resident with intent to stay indefinitely, so most newcomers apply the year after their first full calendar year here. Check the Alaska PFD Division for exact eligibility rules.

What should I do first after moving to Alaska?

Get an Alaska driver's license and register your vehicle with the Alaska DMV, prepare your car and home for winter, and connect with local groups or activities to build community through the dark months.
