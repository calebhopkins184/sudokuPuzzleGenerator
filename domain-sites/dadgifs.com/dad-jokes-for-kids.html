Dad Jokes for Kids: Clean, Silly and School-Safe

The best dad jokes for kids are short, rely on simple puns or sounds they already know, and have a clear “aha” moment. Below are favorites grouped for different ages, from knock-knock jokes for little ones to riddles that make older kids think — plus ideas for turning joke time into a game.

By the Dad GIFs editorial team · Updated November 2, 2026

School jokes

Great for lunchbox notes and the drive to school.

  • Why did the student eat his homework?The teacher said it was a piece of cake.
  • What’s a pirate’s favorite letter?You’d think it’s R, but it’s the C.
  • Why was the broom late for school?It over-swept.
  • What do elves learn in school?The elf-abet.
  • Why did the pencil go to the principal?It wasn’t sharp in class.
  • What did the triangle say to the circle?You’re pointless.
  • Why did the music teacher need a ladder?To reach the high notes.
  • How do bees get to school?On the school buzz.

Animal jokes for kids

Animal puns are often the first jokes children understand, because they already know the animal sounds and names being played with.

  • What do you call a dinosaur that is sleeping?A dino-snore.
  • Why do fish live in salt water?Because pepper makes them sneeze.
  • What does a cat say when it makes a mistake?Me-ow-ps.
  • Why did the chicken join a band?It had the drumsticks.
  • What do you get when you cross a snake and a pie?A pie-thon.
  • What’s a cat’s favorite color?Purr-ple.
  • Where do cows go on Saturday night?To the moo-vies.
  • What kind of tree fits in your hand?A palm tree.

Knock-knock jokes

Knock-knock jokes are ideal for younger children because the structure is predictable: once kids know the pattern, they can join in and even invent their own.

  • Knock, knock. Who’s there? Lettuce. Lettuce who?Lettuce in, it’s cold out here!
  • Knock, knock. Who’s there? Boo. Boo who?Don’t cry, it’s only a joke.
  • Knock, knock. Who’s there? Interrupting cow. Interrupting c—MOO!
  • Knock, knock. Who’s there? Olive. Olive who?Olive you very much.
  • Knock, knock. Who’s there? Atch. Atch who?Bless you!
  • Knock, knock. Who’s there? Tank. Tank who?You’re welcome.

Riddles for older kids

Riddles ask children to think about words with more than one meaning, which makes them a good step up from knock-knocks.

  • What has hands but can’t clap?A clock.
  • What gets wetter the more it dries?A towel.
  • What has to be broken before you can use it?An egg.
  • What has a neck but no head?A bottle.
  • What goes up but never comes down?Your age.
  • What has many keys but can’t open a single lock?A piano.

Matching jokes to ages

What kinds of jokes tend to work at different ages
Age (roughly)What tends to land
3–5Silly sounds, surprises, animal noises, the interrupting cow
5–7Knock-knock jokes, simple puns on familiar words
7–10Riddles, puns with double meanings, school jokes
10+Wordplay, irony, and groaning theatrically at your jokes

Every child is different, so treat this as a loose guide. If a joke needs a long explanation, it’s probably one to save for later.

Why jokes are good for kids

Jokes are a fun way to play with language. Understanding a pun means noticing that a word can sound like another word or mean two different things, and many educators use riddles and wordplay to build vocabulary and listening skills. Telling a joke also takes timing and a little courage, and laughing together is simply a nice way to connect.

Joke-time games for families

  • Joke of the day: one joke at breakfast or in a lunchbox note. Kids can take turns choosing.
  • Don’t laugh challenge: take turns telling jokes; the first person to laugh tells the next one.
  • Finish the punchline: read the setup and let kids invent their own answers. Theirs are often funnier.
  • Joke jar: write jokes on slips of paper for road trips and rainy days.
  • Make-a-pun: pick an object in the room and try to make a pun about it. See our pun-writing guide for easy tricks.

Keeping it kind

Good family jokes laugh at words, not at people. Steer away from jokes that tease someone’s looks, background or abilities, and help kids notice when a joke might hurt someone’s feelings. Dad jokes are perfect here: their target is the language itself.

Quick answers

What is a good dad joke for kids?

Try “What do you call a sleeping dinosaur? A dino-snore.” It is short, uses a word kids know and has an easy-to-see punchline.

At what age do kids understand puns?

Many children begin to enjoy simple puns and knock-knock jokes in the early school years, with riddles and double meanings landing as they get older. Every child develops at their own pace.
