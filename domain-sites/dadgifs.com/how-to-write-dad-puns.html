Pun guide

How to Write Dad Puns (and Deliver Them Shamelessly)

A pun is a joke that exploits a word’s double meaning or its similarity in sound to another word. To write a dad pun, pick a topic, list words connected to it, find a word that sounds like or means something else, and build a short setup that makes the listener expect one meaning before delivering the other.

By the Dad GIFs editorial team · Updated November 2, 2026

The main types of puns

Almost every dad joke uses one of a handful of techniques. Knowing them makes it much easier to write your own.

Pun types with examples
TypeHow it worksExample
Homophone punTwo words sound the same but are spelled differently“The bicycle couldn’t stand up because it was two tired.”
Double meaning (homograph)One word, two meanings“I used to be a banker, but I lost interest.”
Near-sound punWords sound close but not identical“A fake noodle is an impasta.”
Portmanteau / mash-upTwo words blended into one“A pile of cats is a meowtain.”
Literal interpretationTaking a figure of speech literally“My boss said have a good day, so I went home.”
Idiom twistChanging one word in a familiar phrase“Outstanding in his field.”

The five-step pun formula

  1. Pick a topic. Choose something in front of you or relevant to the moment: pizza, a dog, a car wash, a math test.
  2. Brainstorm related words. For pizza: crust, slice, cheese, topping, dough, oven, pepperoni, deliver.
  3. Find the second meaning. Which words have another meaning or sound like other words? Dough (money), slice (golf shot), topping (being the best), cheesy (corny).
  4. Build a misleading setup. Write a question or statement that steers the listener toward the normal meaning. “Why did the pizza chef get rich?”
  5. Reveal the twist. Deliver the second meaning as briefly as possible. “He had a lot of dough.”

Rule of thumb: the pun word should come as close to the end of the punchline as you can manage. The last word is where the surprise lives.

Worked examples

Topic: coffee

Related words: mug, brew, grind, bean, espresso, latte, roast. Double meanings: mugged (robbed), grind (hard work), espresso (sounds like express), roast (to tease). Result: “Why did the coffee call the police? It got mugged.”

Topic: gardening

Related words: weed, plant, soil, root, leaf, hoe, rake, thyme. Sound-alikes: thyme / time, leaf / leave. Result: “I would tell you a gardening joke, but I don’t have the thyme.”

Topic: the ocean

Related words: wave, tide, shore, current, sea, shell. Double meanings: wave (hello), current (up to date), shore / sure. Result: “What did the ocean say to the beach? Nothing, it just waved.”

Delivery: where dad jokes are won

A mediocre pun delivered with confidence beats a brilliant pun delivered nervously. The audience isn’t laughing with the pun; they’re reacting to your shameless pride in it.

  • Deadpan setup. Keep a straight face, as if it’s a genuine question.
  • The pause. Wait a beat before the punchline. Let them try to guess.
  • The reveal. Short and crisp. Don’t over-explain.
  • The reaction. Welcome the groan. A finger-gun or eyebrow raise is optional but encouraged.
  • The follow-up. Experts chain a second pun on the same topic: “Don’t worry, I’ll stop. These jokes are getting stale. Like the bread.”

The ambush pun

Some of the best dad humor isn’t a prepared joke at all — it’s a reflex. The most famous example is “Hi Hungry, I’m Dad,” triggered whenever someone says “I’m…” followed by anything. Other ambush classics:

  • “Can you put my shoes on?” “I don’t think they’ll fit me.”
  • “Dad, can you make me a sandwich?” “Poof, you’re a sandwich.”
  • “Did you get a haircut?” “No, I got them all cut.”
  • “Is the car running?” “No, it’s parked.”

Common mistakes

  • Too much setup. If the joke needs a paragraph, trim it.
  • Explaining the pun. If you have to explain it, move on to the next one.
  • Mean-spirited targets. Dad jokes are wholesome. Punch at words, not people.
  • Obscure words. A pun only works if the audience knows both meanings.

Practice drills

  1. Every morning, pick one object and write three puns about it.
  2. Take a common idiom and change one word: “a piece of cake” becomes “a piece of steak” for a barbecue joke.
  3. Rewrite a joke from our best dad jokes list with a new topic but the same pun type.

Quick answers

What is the easiest way to make a pun?

Pick a topic, list words related to it, and look for one that sounds like or also means something else. Then write a short question that sets up the normal meaning and answer with the twist.

Why do people groan at puns?

The groan is a playful reaction to wordplay that is clever and obvious at the same time. With dad jokes, the groan is part of the fun and a sign the joke worked.
